A dataset and recognition baseline for Assamese numeric sequences. The accompanying research combines 3D convolution, Gabor features, a bidirectional GRU, and attention.

WBNet

A Cascade Framework for Word Boundary Detector and Classifier for Visual Lip Reading

A two-stage approach separates word boundary detection from word classification. The local research summary reports an Assamese sequence word error rate of 26.08%.

Aniket Dewangan, Meghali Deka, Uttam Biswas, Prithwijit Guha, Priyankoo Sarmah

LipKeyFormer

A Lightweight Framework for Landmark-Only Visual Lip Reading in Low-Resource Language

A compact model processes lip landmarks with a Transformer encoder, bidirectional GRU, and temporal attention. The research summary reports 0.38 million parameters and 0.04 GFLOPs for a 50-frame input.

Meghali Deka, Prithwijit Guha, Priyankoo Sarmah

Institutional acceptance record

02 / DATASETS

Language resources for Assamese

112

WORD CLASSES

VLRASN-112

Compound numeric sequences with 300 sequences in the local presentation. Its numeric vocabulary supports studying word boundaries within longer number expressions.

Version note: the deck reports 60 speakers and 6,000 videos; it also notes 30 speakers in the published abstract. These describe different reported snapshots and require release-version confirmation.

1,000

SENTENCES

VLRAS-1000

Everyday three-word sentences. The deck reports 956 word tokens, 250 speakers, and 25,000 videos.

Counts are presentation-reported. An earlier chart totals 252 speakers; the final release manifest and demographic counts remain to be reconciled.

What an annotated sample contains

Each sentence connects synchronized video and audio to word timings, ordered mouth crops, and independent landmark files. The structure below is an illustrative schema, not a downloadable participant recording.

S<number>/
├── output/
│   ├── annotations/   # CTC, manual and selected LAB copies
│   ├── clips/         # synchronized sentence media
│   └── validation/    # review reports and waveform plots
└── visual_dataset/   # ordered mouth frames and landmark CSVs

Preserving the source sequence

Frames retain their original temporal order, with continuous numbering such as 0001. Canonical mouth crops use lossless PNG. Sampling or padding happens later when preparing a model input.

A public sample set will be added after the research owner selects the files and confirms the release terms.

03 / METHODS & ONGOING WORK

From recording to reviewed data

01

Align the session

Align the complete ordered transcript with the full recording using Assamese CTC forced alignment.

02

Review boundaries

Cut between adjacent aligned words at low-energy waveform locations. Reviewers accept, correct, merge, or split clips.

03

Select annotations

Choose reviewed CTC boundaries or validated manual WaveSurfer LAB annotations, retaining both copies and provenance.

04

Extract visual features

MediaPipe produces the canonical mouth crops. MediaPipe and dlib export separate raw and normalized landmarks.

Implemented in the local AVLR Annotation Studio. The project handoff identifies dataset manifests, speaker-independent splits, and model-input sampling as further preparation work.
